Protecting
Your Bitcoin

A plain-English guide to keeping your Bitcoin private and secure — written for busy people who run businesses, not for cryptographers.

Version 1 · August 2026 · Your account manager will walk you through this at your first sweep visit.

You don’t need to understand any of the technical detail in this guide to benefit from it. You just need to follow three steps, in order, when the time is right. Your account manager will sit with you for each one.

This guide exists because the Bitcoin you earn deserves the same care you give your cash till.

Why this matters

When you receive a bank transfer, nobody can see your bank balance. When you receive Bitcoin, the opposite is true by default — every transaction on the Bitcoin network is public. Anyone who knows your Bitcoin address can look it up and see every payment you’ve ever received.

This guide shows you three simple steps to close that gap. None of them are complicated. All of them matter — especially if you’re running a family business and the Bitcoin sits alongside your personal savings.

You are not alone in this

Every step in this guide is something your Refueler account manager will do with you in person. You don’t need to set anything up by yourself.

Three steps, one at a time

Think of Bitcoin privacy like securing a physical premises. You wouldn’t leave the safe open, the back door unlocked, and the alarm off all at once. You deal with each thing in turn. Same principle here.

1
Receiving safely

Use a private address for your savings wallet

When Bitcoin arrives at your savings wallet, it lands at an address. If you give the same address to every customer, anyone can watch that address and see every penny you’ve ever received. It’s like having your bank balance written on a noticeboard outside your shop.

The modern fix is called Silent Payments. You set up your savings wallet once with a single permanent address. Every time Bitcoin arrives, it actually lands at a different, unique address — but only you can find them. Someone looking at the blockchain from the outside sees nothing they can connect back to you.

Your account manager will set this up with you using Sparrow Wallet (see Step 2). No technical knowledge required.

2
Sweeping safely

Use Sparrow Wallet to move Bitcoin to your savings

When you accumulate Bitcoin through your Refueler terminal and want to move it somewhere safer for the long term, that move is called a sweep. A standard sweep is visible on the Bitcoin network — it shows which address sent the money and which received it, which can be used to build a picture of your finances over time.

Sparrow Wallet is a free, well-respected Bitcoin wallet for Mac, Windows, and Linux. When you sweep using Sparrow, it uses a technique called Payjoin: instead of a simple send, both sides of the transaction contribute an input, making it much harder for anyone watching the blockchain to work out what actually happened or who owns what.

We recommend Sparrow Wallet sparrowwallet.com

Your account manager will install Sparrow with you and do your first sweep together. This is the visit that happens once you have accumulated around £200 in Bitcoin and want to move it somewhere secure.

What Payjoin does in plain English

Imagine you’re paying a friend back for dinner and they put a fiver in the envelope too before sealing it. Anyone who opens the envelope later can’t tell who put what in. Payjoin does the same thing with a Bitcoin transaction.

3
Cleaning the history

CoinJoin — when you’re ready to go further

Steps 1 and 2 protect you going forward. But if you have Bitcoin that came from somewhere with a known history — a cryptocurrency exchange that asked for your ID, for example — that history travels with those coins like a paper trail.

CoinJoin is the tool for cleaning that trail. Several people’s Bitcoin is combined into one large transaction and then distributed back out, making it impossible to say whose coins went where. The result is Bitcoin with no traceable history attached.

This is not something you need on day one. It is something to consider once you are comfortable with Steps 1 and 2 and have a meaningful amount saved. Sparrow Wallet supports CoinJoin directly, so no new software is needed when you get there.

No rush on this one

Steps 1 and 2 will take care of the Bitcoin you earn through Refueler from the beginning. Step 3 is for any Bitcoin you already hold with a history attached to it. Your account manager will flag when it’s worth thinking about.

A word about security

As a Bitcoin-accepting business, you may attract more attention from scammers than before. This is not a reason to worry — it is a reason to know the rules. Here they are.

How Refueler will and will never contact you

We will:

  • Email you from hello@refueler.io or support@refueler.io for account and support matters.
  • Contact your account manager directly for anything urgent.
  • Post any service notices at refueler.io/status/ — this is the only place we announce issues publicly.

We will never:

  • Send you a sign-in link you didn’t ask for. Sign-in always starts from you, at the terminal.
  • Ask for your PIN by email, phone, or text.
  • Ask you to move Bitcoin urgently to a new address.
  • Contact you by SMS, WhatsApp, or social media about your account.

If you receive a message that does any of the above, do not act on it. Forward it to support@refueler.io and call your account manager.

In summary

Three steps, in your own time, with your account manager beside you:

  1. Set up Sparrow Wallet as your savings destination — private address from the start.
  2. Do your first sweep once you have around £200 accumulated — your account manager will walk you through it.
  3. Consider CoinJoin when you have older Bitcoin with a history you’d rather not carry. Sparrow handles it when you’re ready.

None of this requires you to become a Bitcoin expert. It just requires doing it once, with someone who already knows how.
